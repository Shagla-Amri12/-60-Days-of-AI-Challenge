🔍 How this was researched

Data collection approach

Each vehicle's specs were pulled directly from the manufacturer, EPA-derived range figures reported by Kelley Blue Book / Edmunds / Cars.com / US News, and official safety-testing bodies (NHTSA, IIHS). Where a number is a manufacturer estimate rather than an EPA-certified or independently tested figure, it is flagged as an ESTIMATE. Where no reliable figure exists yet, it is flagged as a DATA GAP.

Conflicts & how they were resolved

  • Tesla Model 3 trim confusion: Sources vary depending on whether they cite the base "Standard" trim or the longer-range "Premium RWD" trim. This tool uses the base Standard trim ($38,380 MSRP / 363-mi range per KBB's official spec sheet) since it's the most relevant configuration for a budget-conscious first-time buyer.
  • Chevrolet Bolt range discrepancy: The relaunched Bolt (technically a 2027 model year vehicle beginning deliveries in early 2026) has been reported with EPA range estimates ranging from 255 to 262 miles across pre-launch coverage. GM's own newsroom release confirms pricing but has not published a final EPA-certified range figure as of this writing. That range is flagged as an ESTIMATE pending final EPA certification.
  • Chevrolet Bolt cost-to-own and safety rating: Because the relaunched Bolt had not begun customer deliveries at time of writing, Kelley Blue Book has no 5-Year Cost to Own figure, and neither NHTSA nor IIHS has crash-tested it yet. Both are marked as DATA GAPS — treat the Bolt's cost/safety ranking with extra caution until real-world and test data exists.
  • Volkswagen ID.4 cost-to-own: KBB's specific 2026 ID.4 Cost to Own page did not return a public dollar figure in available search results at time of writing. Rather than invent a number, this tool marks it as an ESTIMATE, interpolated from the ID.4's 2025 KBB cost-to-own history and its close price/segment overlap with the Ioniq 5. Verify directly on kbb.com before relying on it.
  • Volkswagen ID.4 safety rating: Neither NHTSA nor IIHS had a confirmed, published 2026-model-year overall rating in available sources at time of writing (the ID.4 has been well-rated in prior model years, e.g., IIHS "Top Safety Pick" history), so its current-year rating is flagged as a DATA GAP pending fresh testing confirmation.
  • Hyundai Ioniq 5 NHTSA rating: US News reported that NHTSA had not completed full testing of the 2026 Ioniq 5 at time of writing, even though IIHS awarded it Top Safety Pick+. This is noted directly in the table rather than assumed.

What "5-Year Cost to Own" includes

Per Kelley Blue Book's methodology, this figure combines projected out-of-pocket expenses (fuel/electricity, insurance, maintenance, repairs, financing, and state fees) with expected 5-year depreciation. It is a KBB-modeled estimate for a typical owner/location, not a guaranteed cost — your insurance, electricity rates, and financing terms will change your actual number.

Limitations to keep in mind

  • MSRPs exclude destination fees, taxes, title, and dealer add-ons unless otherwise noted.
  • The federal EV tax credit expired September 30, 2025 and is not reflected in any listed price — factor in any remaining state/local incentives separately.
  • The Chevrolet Bolt is the newest and least-verified entry here since it only began shipping to customers in early 2026 — treat its figures as more provisional than the other three.

This tool is for general research/education purposes and reflects publicly available data as of July 2026. It is not financial or purchasing advice — verify current pricing, incentives, and availability with dealers and official sources before buying.
