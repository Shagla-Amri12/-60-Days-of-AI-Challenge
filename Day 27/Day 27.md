# Day 27/60 — Claude AI Challenge:
# Prior Authorization Workflow Simulator:

This project is an interactive simulator that walks through a realistic Prior Authorization (PA) journey for a specialty medication — from prescription to denial, appeal, and final approval.

The goal was to translate a complex healthcare operations process into a clear, explorable workflow that highlights where delays happen and why.

# Overview:

Prior Authorization is one of the most opaque and frustrating parts of healthcare delivery.
This simulator models how a PA request moves across Patient → Provider → Payer, using a story-driven approach to show real-world decision points and failure modes.

The simulator is educational, not tied to any real payer or patient data.

# Objective:
* Demystify the Prior Authorization process for non-clinical audiences
* Visualize how documentation gaps impact care timelines
* Show how workflow design directly affects patient outcomes
* Learn healthcare operations through hands-on simulation, not theory

# Key Learnings:

* Most PA denials are operational, not clinical
* Step therapy documentation is the single biggest denial driver for specialty drugs
* A denial is often a request for missing information, not a final decision
* Appeals significantly increase approval chances when documentation is corrected
* Administrative rework creates real cost and treatment delays
  
# One New Thing I Learned:

A single missing data element (like proof of prior first-line therapy) can delay treatment by weeks — even when the medication is medically appropriate.

Prior Authorization is less about deciding care and more about verifying that the right evidence exists in the right format at the right time.

# Why This Matters:

For progressive conditions, workflow inefficiencies aren’t just inconvenient — they can worsen outcomes.
This project reinforced how better system design and automation could remove friction for both providers and patients.
